# Study instructions

Before preparing, changing, analyzing, or planning a study, read the
[study guide](README.md), including its [research workflows](README.md#research-workflows).

- Start with the research question and the decision it informs. Follow linked
  evidence as needed for the task. State what was inspected and what was
  unavailable; distinguish reporting a retained finding from independently
  verifying it. New numerical claims require retained measurements and
  reproducible calculations.
- State the focal comparison before interpreting its numbers: which policies
  changed or stayed fixed, training budgets, selected checkpoints, opponents,
  and evaluation cases. Check consequential differences in environment, rewards,
  observations, and evaluation procedure before combining results.
- Use the study's saved selection and retained analyzer to check compatibility
  and completeness. Apply the guide's rules for independent training replicates,
  missing cases, and exclusions. Record or hash validation alone does not
  establish that a comparison answers the scientific question.
- Separate measurements, interpretations, and hypotheses. Label analyses
  developed after inspecting results as exploratory. Check prevalence and
  counterexamples before generalizing a failure; a single case can still be
  investigated as one case. Treat behavioral explanations as hypotheses unless
  evidence distinguishes plausible alternatives.
- Check that the executed method and analysis implement the intended comparison.
  Preserve the original selection rule and any amendment, its rationale and
  timing, and its effect on the conclusion. A retry, resume, or salvaged
  checkpoint does not add an independent training replicate.
- Reconcile dated summaries with newer evidence while preserving historical
  selections and reports. Record consequential changes in understanding and
  link related studies; keep operational chronology in execution records.
- Prefer analysis or evaluation of existing policies when sufficient. Propose
  bounded next steps with expected learning, compute and storage costs, and
  stopping conditions. Execute within the applicable authorization and resource
  envelope; a proposed experiment is not a launch instruction.
